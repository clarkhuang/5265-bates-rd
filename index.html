Matte charcoal-black shingles

Vinyl-safe paint The siding is vinyl. Light colors such as Alabaster are generally safe to use on vinyl. Darker body colors can warp or buckle vinyl unless they are mixed in a vinyl-safe formula, such as Sherwin-Williams VinylSafe. Regular Iron Ore (SW 7069) and Tricorn Black (SW 6258) are not VinylSafe body colors; do not use them on the siding itself. If you prefer the darker alternate below, ask the paint store for VinylSafe Pepper Shaker (SWVS 435), a dark charcoal in that family, not a standard match of Iron Ore.

Barn concept

A 60×50 pole barn (3,000 sf) with a 16 ft eave, planned as a shop, vehicle barn, and full CrossFit gym, at the sandy circular turnaround to the left of and behind the house. That clearing is on the 5 acres. The existing drive and loop stay and are paved, including the tree island in the middle of the loop. The playground west of the house — the playset, trampoline, fenced play area, and the small green-roofed play structure — comes out. The garden shed north of the house is not part of that.

The barn has two 12×14 insulated overhead doors and a walk-in door on the front, a 12×10 overhead on the gym side (14×10 is a modest upgrade), a 32×24 ft outdoor workout pad, a 4–5 in. slab, 200 amp electrical, and a gym-style finish: insulation, plywood to 8 ft, and steel liner above. The ceiling stays unfinished, with the trusses left exposed.

At 3,000 sf the plan fits an Airstream Interstate 24X (~24.5 ft long, 9 ft 9 in tall) in a 16.5×50 ft bay, a fun-car stall, and about 1,400 sf of gym for a 4-person rig, platforms, a wall rack, and cardio. A 16 ft eave gives comfortable clearance at a 12–14 ft door and at the rig.

Placement at the west loop

When you face the garage, front is south, left is west, back is north, and right is east. The east paved teardrop at the garage is not the barn site. The west edge of the sand sits at about the 40-ft rear setback, so a 60×50 barn does not fit fully west of the loop. Two placements keep the loop and open the vehicle doors onto it.

Option A (recommended) sits on the west edge of the clearing and becomes the west side of the paved court. The two 12×14 doors face east onto the loop, with about 40 ft of apron to the tree island. The gym door is on the south wall, opening to the outdoor pad. Setbacks work. The pad is not flat: county elevation data shows about a 14 ft spread across the footprint, so it will need real cut and fill, but it is the milder of the two options.

Option B sits just north of the loop, in trees, with the doors facing south onto the paved circle. Setbacks are comfortable, but the hill drops hard to the north — about a 23 ft spread and a slope around 27%. Expect substantial cut/fill or a retaining wall, and use the high end of the site-prep range.

Plywood vs drywall, and the ceiling

Plywood costs more than drywall — typically about $3,000–$6,000 more for this barn — but it is the better gym wall. It takes a hit from a wall ball, and you can screw a rack or rig into it anywhere. Drywall has to be taped, mudded, and painted, and it dents. A common compromise, and the spec above, is plywood to 8 ft with white steel liner panels above.

Ceiling option A (recommended for this spec): closed-cell spray foam on the underside of the roof steel, with the trusses left exposed. That keeps the vault, about 22–24 ft at the peak, and stops condensation on the steel. It costs more, about $15,000–$33,000 including a thermal-barrier coating, because a gym is occupied space.

Ceiling option B: a white steel liner on the bottom of the trusses, with blown-in insulation above. Usually a bit cheaper (about $12,000–$25,000). You get a clean flat ceiling at about eave height, around 16 ft, and you lose the vault. If you chose B instead of A, subtract roughly $3,000–$8,000 from the typical barn total.

To verify before bidding

  • A survey of the house, the west loop and tree island, the playground, the grade at both pads, and the parcel corners.
  • The well and septic locations, before any digging or paving. They are not in public GIS.
  • Acme Township land-use review: agricultural zoning, setbacks (50 ft front, 25 ft sides, 40 ft rear, 10 ft from the house), and that a ~24 ft peak stays under the house height. An accessory land-use permit is $50, plus county building and electrical permits.
  • The recorded driveway easement — width, rights for construction traffic, maintenance, and any restrictions on paving or widening the shared section.
  • A builder’s site visit for cut and fill, especially if you compare Option A and Option B, and for the outdoor pad.
  • Whether the house electrical service can feed a 200 amp barn subpanel without an upgrade, given a trench of about 240–290 ft.
