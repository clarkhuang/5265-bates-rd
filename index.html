New architectural shingles

Matte charcoal-black shingles

Vinyl-safe paint The siding is vinyl. Light colors such as Alabaster are generally safe to use on vinyl. Darker body colors can warp or buckle vinyl unless they are mixed in a vinyl-safe formula, such as Sherwin-Williams VinylSafe. Regular Iron Ore (SW 7069) and Tricorn Black (SW 6258) are not VinylSafe body colors; do not use them on the siding itself. If you prefer the darker alternate below, ask the paint store for VinylSafe Pepper Shaker (SWVS 435), a dark charcoal in that family, not a standard match of Iron Ore.

Barn concept

Site plan is being revised. The barn belongs at the circular turnaround to the left of and behind the house. A revised fit check is in progress, so placement maps are not shown here yet. The building specification, interior layout, and cost ranges below still apply.

A 60×50 pole barn (3,000 sf) with a 16 ft eave, planned as a shop, vehicle barn, and full CrossFit gym. Two 12×14 insulated overhead doors and a walk-in door on the front, a 10×10 overhead on the back or side, a 4–5 in. slab, 200 amp electrical, and a gym-style finish: insulation, plywood to 8 ft, and steel liner above. The ceiling stays unfinished, with the trusses left exposed.

At 3,000 sf the plan fits an Airstream Interstate 24X (~24.5 ft long, 9 ft 9 in tall) in a 16.5×50 ft bay, a fun-car stall, and about 1,400 sf of gym for a 4-person rig, platforms, a wall rack, and cardio. A 16 ft eave gives comfortable clearance at a 12–14 ft door and at the rig.

Plywood vs drywall, and the ceiling

Plywood costs more than drywall — typically about $3,000–$6,000 more for this barn — but it is the better gym wall. It takes a hit from a wall ball, and you can screw a rack or rig into it anywhere. Drywall has to be taped, mudded, and painted, and it dents. A common compromise, and the spec above, is plywood to 8 ft with white steel liner panels above.

Ceiling option A (recommended for this spec): closed-cell spray foam on the underside of the roof steel, with the trusses left exposed. That keeps the vault, about 22–24 ft at the peak, and stops condensation on the steel. It costs more, about $15,000–$33,000 including a thermal-barrier coating, because a gym is occupied space.

Ceiling option B: a white steel liner on the bottom of the trusses, with blown-in insulation above. Usually a bit cheaper (about $12,000–$25,000). You get a clean flat ceiling at about eave height, around 16 ft, and you lose the vault. If you chose B instead of A, subtract roughly $3,000–$8,000 from the typical total.

To verify before bidding

  • A survey of the house, the circular turnaround, and the grade at that pad.
  • The well and septic locations, before any digging. They are not in public GIS.
  • Acme Township land-use review: agricultural zoning, setbacks, and that a ~24 ft peak stays under the house height. An accessory land-use permit is $50, plus county building and electrical permits.
  • The recorded driveway easement — width, rights for construction traffic, and any restrictions.
  • A builder’s site visit for cut, fill, and the best wall for the 10×10 door.
  • Whether the house electrical service can feed a 200 amp barn subpanel without an upgrade.
